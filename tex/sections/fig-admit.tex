      {\scriptsize A step carrying riders is \emph{already} slower.}\\
      {\scriptsize Measure $T_{\text{hide}}$ on such a step and the}\\
      {\scriptsize budget grows to justify the riders that}\\
      {\scriptsize made it grow. So $T_{\text{hide}}$ is measured}\\

\caption{\textbf{Deciding where a request lives.} Because a request's context length
scales what it costs in device memory \emph{and} what it costs in link traffic, it
cancels out of the comparison and cannot discriminate between the tiers. What is
actually scarce is link time within one step, so admission is a bandwidth test: the
cohort's bytes must fit the time a step can hide. Two failure modes shape the rest of
the logic --- a budget measured on a rider-carrying step validates itself, and a
decision that is legal in bytes can still be wrong in latency, so it is made
provisionally and withdrawn if the measurement disagrees.}
