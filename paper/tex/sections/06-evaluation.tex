\subsection{Riders are additive}
\label{sec:eval:capacity}

The first question is whether a host-tier request can be served \emph{in
addition to} a full HBM-resident cohort rather than in place of part of it.
At a fixed KV pool, with \texttt{b\_hbm\_max} set to the measured all-HBM
ceiling:

offered & all-HBM peak running & mixture peak running \\
\midrule
48 & 8 & \textbf{48} \\
96 & 7 & \textbf{96} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{HBM is no longer the binding constraint; host memory and the
interconnect are. Both mixture figures are \textbf{lower bounds} --- the
mixture held the entire offered load, so its own ceiling was never reached.

\subsection{What a rider costs}
\label{sec:eval:cost}

\todo{THE LOAD-BEARING SUBSECTION. Figure F4.} Sweeping rider count on an
otherwise identical engine and fitting
$\mathrm{ITL}(r) = \mathrm{ITL}_0 + \mathrm{fixed} + r\cdot(\text{bytes}/B_{\text{eff}})$
separates a fixed per-step cost of using the host path at all from a per-byte
cost that fails to overlap. The two extrapolate to completely different answers
on a faster link, which is why a single rider count cannot decide the question.

\subsection{The weight-precision pair}
\label{sec:eval:precision}

\needsgh{GH200 plan 4.4b, 6 GPU-h. Qwen-32B at fp8 and fp16: same architecture,
same tokenizer, same KV geometry, half the weight footprint. $\Ckv$ goes

RATIO between precisions, not the absolute numbers.}
