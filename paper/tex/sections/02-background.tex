\section{Background and system model}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Capacity, not compute, caps the batch}
\label{sec:background:capacity}

A decode step is memory-bound by a wide margin: it reads every weight and every
resident KV byte once, at an arithmetic intensity equal to the GQA group size
(4--8 on current models) against an \emph{achieved} balance point of order 100
FLOP/byte (Appendix~\ref{app:params}). This is the premise the offloading
literature is built on --- FlexGen~\cite{flexgen}, Pie~\cite{pie} and
DirectKV~\cite{directkv} all exploit it --- and it is not unanimous:
NanoFlow~\cite{nanoflow} argues that end-to-end serving becomes
\emph{compute}-bound once operations of different resource classes are
overlapped within a device. The two claims are about different batch sizes, and
\S\ref{sec:related} gives the crossing point we measure. So a decode engine runs at single-digit
arithmetic utilization, and what caps its batch is bytes rather than arithmetic
--- KV exhausts the device long before the streaming multiprocessors (SMs)
saturate. A trace-driven study of KV offloading reaches the same conclusion from
the workload side, finding device memory capacity rather than token budget to be
what binds~\cite{bottlenecks}.

\textbf{Which of the two reads dominates is a property of the model, not of the
operating point}, and the spread is large: at a full pool on an A100-80,
Llama-3.1-8B spends $11.1$ of $78$\,ms on weights (14\% weights) where
Qwen2.5-32B spends $45.2$ of $53$ (85\% weights). Both are capacity-bound, on
identical hardware, at opposite ends of one axis. The weight terms follow from
the measured $1935$\,GB/s peak; the step times they divide are projected
(\S\ref{sec:model:modeldep}).

That spread is what makes a second tier worth having, and its direction is the
opposite of the intuitive one. The weight read is a fixed per-step window in
which the link carries nothing. The larger the weights relative to the device,
the longer that window and the less HBM is left for KV --- so configurations
where weights dominate have both the most idle link time and the least device
memory. Qwen2.5-32B is favourable \emph{because} it is 85\% weights;
Llama-3.1-8B is unfavourable because it is 14\%. \S\ref{sec:model} makes this
quantitative and \S\ref{sec:model:omits} gives the cost that can cancel it.

\subsection{Host memory as a tier for actively decoding requests}

Offloading KV to host memory is an old idea that a new interconnect has changed.
Over PCIe~4 at roughly 25\,GB/s, streaming a request's KV every decode step
costs more than the step itself, so host memory could only be a \emph{swap}
target: somewhere to park a request that is not running. NVLink-C2C is specified
at 450\,GB/s per direction and measured at 419~\cite{pie}; at that rate the KV a
decoding request needs can cross the link inside the window the GPU spends
reading its own weights.

That makes host memory a tier an \emph{actively decoding} request can occupy.
Several systems already decode from host-resident KV
(\S\ref{sec:related}); what none of them does is \emph{choose, per request,}
which tier such a request lives in --- continuous batching, prefix reuse,
eviction and hierarchical caching all decide residency for requests that are
\emph{not} currently running.

\paragraph{Host DRAM is the first such tier, not the only one.} The direction of
travel is towards accelerators attached to more than one kind of memory:
CXL-attached DRAM behind a coherent link, and high-bandwidth flash placed
between DRAM and storage, are both being built for exactly the capacity problem
\S\ref{sec:background:capacity} describes. Nothing in this design is specific to
host DRAM. What it asks of a tier is three things, and they are the right
questions to ask of any of them: that the tier be larger than device memory,
that the path to it have bandwidth we can \emph{measure} at runtime, and that a
request's per-step traffic over that path fit inside the compute of one
layer --- which is what decides whether the tier can hold \emph{decoding}
requests or only parked ones. The capacity model of \S\ref{sec:model} is written
in those three quantities and nothing else, so it transfers to a new tier by
re-profiling rather than by re-deriving; \S\ref{sec:discussion:general} works
through what changes when a third tier is added below the second.

It also changes what the movement should be. \emph{Swapping} relocates
ownership: a unit lives on one side, is brought over, and must be written back
if it changed, so every offloaded byte crosses the link twice per generated
token. \emph{Streaming} keeps the host copy authoritative and treats the device
copy as a cache, so the return leg carries only the token just produced. That is
a factor of two on the resource which bounds the whole technique, and it follows
from what the offloaded unit \emph{is} rather than from any optimisation;
\S\ref{sec:related} works it through against the systems that made each choice.

\subsection{System model: a disaggregated decode node}
\label{sec:background:pd}

We target a \textbf{decode node in a prefill/decode-disaggregated
deployment}~\cite{distserve,splitwise,mooncake}: prefill runs on separate
hardware, and a request arrives with its KV already computed. This deployment is
widely used and supported by the major serving stacks, because prefill and
decode have opposite resource profiles --- though colocated chunked-prefill
serving remains common, and our own negative result below is that the technique
does not apply there.

The assumption does real work. On a decode node a host-tier request's KV can be
delivered \emph{into host memory} and never occupy an HBM block, not even
transiently, so riders are \textbf{additive}: served in addition to a full
resident cohort rather than displacing part of it.

In a combined prefill+decode engine this is false, and not subtly. A request
must be prefilled somewhere, so its KV lands in HBM first and is reclaimed only
afterwards; riders and residents then draw on the same headroom. We measured the
result: the served total creeps toward the all-HBM capacity from below and never
passes it, at any setting of the resident cap, because a rider whose prefill
stays resident frees nothing while still paying interconnect every step
(\S\ref{sec:eval:capacity}).

The cost of the assumption is that KV must reach host DRAM.
\S\ref{sec:impl:stash} implements that handover and measures it, so the reader
can price the assumption rather than accept it.
